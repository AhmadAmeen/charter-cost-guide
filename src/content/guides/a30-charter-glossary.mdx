Charter brochures and quotes are full of abbreviations: APA, FBO, MYBA, deadhead, plus expenses. They all mean specific things, and misunderstanding one can cost real money. This glossary defines the terms you will actually meet when booking a yacht or a private jet, in plain English, one line each. Where a term has a full guide on this site, the term links to it.

### A

**Advance Provisioning Allowance (APA)** — a deposit for the running costs of a yacht charter (fuel, food, drink, dockage), typically 25 to 40% of the base fee; unspent money is returned. See [how charter costs break down](/yacht-charter/cost/).

**Air taxi** — a small aircraft charter for short hops, usually on turboprops or very light jets.

**All-inclusive (charter)** — a pricing basis where most running costs are bundled into the quoted rate, as opposed to [plus expenses](/yacht-charter/cost/).

### B

**Bareboat** — chartering a yacht without crew, so you (or your hired skipper) run it; you need the required licence for the cruising area.

**Base fee** — the advertised hire rate for the yacht and its crew, before APA, taxes and tip. See [the cost breakdown](/yacht-charter/cost/).

**Block hours** — a pre-purchased bundle of flight hours on private jets, sometimes offered at a fixed hourly rate.

**Broker (charter broker)** — an intermediary who arranges charters across many yachts or aircraft; typically paid commission out of the charter fee. See [how brokers get paid](/yacht-charter/how-brokers-get-paid/).

### C

**Cabin (jet)** — the passenger compartment of the aircraft; cabin size is a key difference between jet categories.

**Captain** — the person in command of the yacht, responsible for the vessel, the crew and your safety.

**Catamaran** — a twin-hulled yacht, prized for deck space and stability. See [catamaran vs motor vs sailing yacht](/yacht-charter/catamaran-vs-motor-vs-sailing/).

**Charter agreement (contract)** — the written contract for your trip, covering payments, cancellation, and responsibilities; ask for it before paying any deposit.

**Charter fee** — the total price you pay for the charter, as distinct from the base fee before extras.

**Co-brokerage** — when two brokers cooperate on one booking (for example, your broker and the yacht's listing broker), usually splitting the commission.

**Crew** — the professional team running a crewed yacht: captain, chef, steward/ess, deckhand and sometimes more on larger yachts.

**Crewed charter** — a charter that includes a professional crew; you are a guest, not the operator.

**CYBA** — the Charter Yacht Brokers Association; its agreement form is commonly used for Caribbean charters, while MYBA dominates the Mediterranean.

### D

**Deadhead** — an empty positioning flight; see positioning flight and [empty leg](/private-jets/cost/).

**Delivery fee** — a charge when a yacht must travel outside its home area to reach you; one of the classic [hidden costs](/yacht-charter/hidden-costs-checklist/).

### E

**Empty leg** — a flight an operator must fly anyway (usually repositioning), often sold at 25 to 75% below full price; route, date and time are fixed. See [private jet costs](/private-jets/cost/).

### F

**FBO (Fixed-Base Operator)** — the private terminal and ground-services company at an airport serving business aviation: lounges, fuel, handling and parking.

**Ferry flight** — see positioning flight.

**Fractional ownership** — buying a share of an aircraft (for example, 1/16th) entitling you to a set number of flight hours per year.

### G

**Gratuity (tip)** — discretionary, but around 10% of the base fee is commonly suggested for yacht crew; jet tipping norms vary. See [yacht costs](/yacht-charter/cost/) and [jet tipping etiquette](/private-jets/tipping-guide/).

**Gulet** — a traditional wooden motor-sailer, common for charters in Turkey, Greece and Croatia, usually offered on semi-inclusive terms.

### H

**Heavy jet** — a large-cabin private jet category (about 10–16 seats), suited to 5+ hour and transatlantic flights; indicative rates run about $7,000 to $13,000 per flight hour.

**Home port (home marina)** — where a yacht is normally based; charters starting elsewhere may incur a delivery fee.

### I

**Itinerary** — your planned route and schedule; on crewed yachts it is flexible and agreed with the captain, weather permitting.

### J

**Jet card** — a prepaid card for a fixed number of flight hours at agreed rates, a middle ground between on-demand charter and ownership.

### L

**Light jet** — the smallest jet category (about 6–8 seats), for trips under roughly 3 hours; indicative rates run about $3,500 to $6,500 per flight hour. See [private jet costs](/private-jets/cost/).

### M

**Marina fees (dockage)** — charges for berthing in marinas, normally paid from the APA on a yacht charter.

**Midsize jet** — a jet category (about 7–9 seats) for 3 to 5 hour flights, such as US transcontinental trips.

**Motor yacht** — a yacht driven by engines rather than sail; faster and more spacious per foot, with higher fuel costs. See [catamaran vs motor vs sailing](/yacht-charter/catamaran-vs-motor-vs-sailing/).

**MYBA** — The Worldwide Yachting Association (formerly the Mediterranean Yacht Brokers Association); publishes the standard contract for most larger Mediterranean crewed charters.

### O

**On-demand charter** — booking a jet or yacht trip by trip, paying per flight or per week, with no ownership or membership commitment.

**Operator (aircraft operator)** — the company that owns or manages the aircraft and holds the operating certificate; distinct from a broker who only arranges the booking. See [the jet safety checklist](/private-jets/charter-safety-checklist/).

### P

**Part 135** — the US Federal Aviation Administration certification under which on-demand charter operators fly paying passengers; asking an operator about its certification is a basic safety check.

**Passenger manifest** — the list of everyone travelling, with ID details, which the operator needs before departure.

**Plus expenses** — a pricing basis where the base fee is quoted and running costs are paid separately, usually via the APA. See [yacht costs](/yacht-charter/cost/).

**Positioning flight** — an empty flight to get the aircraft to your departure airport (or back to base); often billed to the charterer. See [how jet pricing works](/private-jets/cost/).

**Preference sheet** — the form where you tell a yacht's crew your food, drink and activity preferences before the trip.

### S

**Sailing yacht** — a yacht primarily wind-powered; the most economical way to charter per week, but slower and more hands-on. See [catamaran vs motor vs sailing](/yacht-charter/catamaran-vs-motor-vs-sailing/).

**Security deposit (bareboat)** — a refundable deposit held against damage on a bareboat charter, separate from the charter fee.

**Skipper** — a hired professional who runs a bareboat yacht for you if you lack the licence or inclination; typically about 180 to 250 euros per day according to a 2026 charter guide.

**Stakeholder** — the neutral party (often the broker) who holds charter funds under a MYBA agreement until they are due to the owner.

**Super-midsize jet** — between midsize and heavy: more range and cabin space than a midsize jet without heavy-jet operating costs.

**Superyacht** — generally a yacht over about 24 metres (80 ft), with a larger crew and full-service operation.

### T

**Tail number** — an aircraft's registration identifier (like a licence plate); ask for it to verify exactly which aircraft you are quoted.

**Tender** — the small boat carried by a yacht for shuttling guests ashore and for water sports.

**Turboprop** — a propeller aircraft with a turbine engine; the most economical private aircraft for short regional hops.

### U

**Ultra-long-range jet** — the largest jet category, capable of nonstop intercontinental flights of 12+ hours.

### V

**VAT (charter tax)** — value-added or charter tax added on top of the base fee in many countries; published samples cite 22% in Italy, 20% in France and 13% in Croatia.

### W

**Water toys** — jet skis, paddleboards, seabobs and similar equipment; check whether they are included or an extra, as they often are on the [hidden costs](/yacht-charter/hidden-costs-checklist/) list.
