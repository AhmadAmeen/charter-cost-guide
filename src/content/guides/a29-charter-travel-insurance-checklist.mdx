A charter trip concentrates a lot of non-refundable money into a few days: deposits, balances, flights to the marina or airport. Insurance does not make that money safe — it moves specific, listed risks to an insurer. This guide walks through what to check before buying a policy for a yacht or private jet trip. It is general information, not advice and not a recommendation of any product. The only document that decides what you are covered for is the policy wording itself.

<Callout type="note" label="Not advice">
We are not insurance advisers and this is not a recommendation to buy or skip any policy. If the wording is unclear, ask the insurer directly and get the answer in writing before you rely on it.
</Callout>

## What the provider's own cover includes

Before shopping for a policy, ask the charter provider what is already covered — you may be paying to duplicate it.

- **The yacht or aircraft** is insured by its owner or operator; that covers their asset, not your deposits or your medical bills.
- **Crew liability** covers injuries the crew might cause in operating the vessel or aircraft — not your trip costs.
- **Your deposits** are generally not insured by the provider at all. If you cancel, the contract's cancellation terms decide what you get back, and the balance is your loss unless a policy covers it.

Ask the provider for these answers in writing before you pay any deposit; verbal reassurance is not cover.

## Trip cancellation cover

Trip cancellation insurance reimburses prepaid, non-refundable trip costs if you cancel for a covered reason. The U.S. State Department describes it plainly: it helps you get your money back if you need to cancel or change plans, but it usually does not pay for medical costs abroad — that is a separate benefit.

What to check:

- **Insured trip cost.** The policy reimburses up to the trip cost you declared. If you insure $5,000 of a $70,000 charter, the payout is capped at $5,000. Declare the full non-refundable amount or accept the gap.
- **Covered reasons list.** Cancellation is only covered for the reasons the policy names (illness, injury, death in the family and similar). "We changed our minds" is not on the list on a standard policy.
- **Timing.** Buy it when you start committing non-refundable money. Some benefits (such as waivers for pre-existing conditions) are only available if you buy within a set window after the first deposit — ask the insurer what that window is.

## Medical and evacuation

This is the section the U.S. State Department emphasises most. Its guidance makes three points worth quoting in spirit: it recommends buying travel health insurance before the trip; it notes that U.S. Medicare and Medicaid do not pay for medical care outside the United States, so travellers should check whether their own health plan covers them abroad; and it strongly recommends medical evacuation insurance, which can be bought separately or as a policy add-on.

What to check:

- **Does your existing health plan cover you abroad?** Ask your provider directly — do not assume.
- **Emergency medical limit.** Hospital care abroad is billed at local private rates, which vary wildly. Check the limit is realistic for your destination.
- **Medical evacuation as its own benefit.** Air-ambulance evacuation is one of the most expensive things that can happen on a trip, which is why it is usually a separately stated benefit with its own limit. Confirm it exists in the policy rather than assuming it is bundled.
- **Adventure activities.** If your charter includes diving, jet skis or similar, check whether the policy treats them as excluded hazardous activities.

## Damage deposit cover on bareboat charters

Bareboat charters hold a refundable security deposit against damage — often several thousand euros. Some travel policies offer cover that can reduce your exposure to that deposit; others exclude it entirely.

Do not guess from the marketing summary. Ask the insurer two specific questions: does the policy cover a bareboat charter security deposit, and if so, is it reimbursement after the charter company deducts, or something else? Get the answer with reference to the policy section number. If the insurer cannot point to the section, treat the answer as no.

## Exclusions to read carefully

Every travel insurance policy has an exclusions section — in sample policy documents it is typically a standalone section near the end, which tells you how seriously insurers take it. Read it before you buy. The usual suspects:

- **Pre-existing medical conditions**, unless a specific waiver was bought in time.
- **Extreme or hazardous sports**, which can include diving and motorised water sports common on charters.
- **Travel against government advice** — if your country's foreign office advises against travel to the destination, cover may be void.
- **Foreseeable events** — a storm that was already named before you bought the policy, for example.
- **Alcohol and substance-related incidents**, which appear in most exclusion lists.

The pattern is consistent: the policy covers the unexpected, not the known. If you already know about the risk when you buy, assume it is excluded until the wording says otherwise.

## Questions for an insurer

Ask these before paying for the policy, and keep the answers:

1. What is the maximum I can claim for trip cancellation, and does it match my total non-refundable charter costs?
2. Which cancellation reasons are covered? Is "cancel for any reason" available, and what does it actually pay?
3. Does my existing health insurance cover me in the destination country? (Ask your health provider, not the travel insurer.)
4. Is medical evacuation a separate benefit with its own limit?
5. Are the water sports and activities on my itinerary excluded as hazardous?
6. Does the policy cover a bareboat security deposit, and under which section?
7. What is the deadline after my first deposit for any time-sensitive benefits?
8. What documents will a claim require — and can I produce them from abroad?

## The checklist

| Check | Done? |
|-------|-------|
| Asked the charter provider what their own cover includes, in writing | ☐ |
| Totalled all non-refundable deposits and balances | ☐ |
| Confirmed whether my health plan covers me abroad | ☐ |
| Compared the policy's cancellation limit against my non-refundable total | ☐ |
| Read the covered-reasons list for cancellation | ☐ |
| Confirmed medical evacuation is a stated benefit with its own limit | ☐ |
| Checked water sports and activities against the hazardous-activities exclusion | ☐ |
| Asked about bareboat security deposit cover, with a policy section reference | ☐ |
| Read the full exclusions section before buying | ☐ |
| Saved the insurer's emergency contact and policy number where the group can find them | ☐ |

Buy the cover when the money becomes non-refundable, keep the policy number with your travel documents, and remember the only promise that matters is the one printed in the wording.
