The Mediterranean is where most first-time charterers start, and for good reason: a week is enough to see a whole coastline, the season is long, and the cost data is the clearest published anywhere. This guide compares the four regions that matter — France, Italy, Croatia and Greece — by cost, taxes, season and trip style, so you can shortlist with numbers instead of vibes.

If you are still choosing between the three eastern heavyweights, our [Greece vs Croatia vs Italy comparison](/yacht-charter/greece-vs-croatia-vs-italy/) goes deeper. For month-by-month season detail, see [best time to charter by destination](/planning/best-time-to-charter-by-destination/).

## The regions

Four regions cover nearly all Mediterranean charter demand:

- **France — the French Riviera.** Monaco, Cannes, St. Tropez, Cap Ferrat. The most visible cruising ground in yachting: marquee harbours, short hops, beach clubs, and an event calendar (Monaco Grand Prix, Cannes Film Festival) that books yachts early. Suits guests who want iconic stops and a lively social scene within close cruising distance.
- **Italy — Amalfi, Sardinia, Sicily, the Aeolian Islands.** Culture, coastline and food, from the pastel cliff villages of the Amalfi Coast to the quieter anchorages of Sardinia. Suits a mix of sightseeing and swimming, with more variety of pace than the Riviera.
- **Croatia — the Dalmatian Coast.** Over a thousand islands, historic coastal towns, national parks, and calm cruising water. Suits island-hopping at a relaxed pace, often at a lower total cost than the western Med.
- **Greece — the Cyclades, Ionian, Dodecanese.** The widest island variety in the Mediterranean: the iconic Cyclades, greener and calmer Ionian water, history in the Dodecanese. Suits longer, slower charters — the trade-off is distance, with longer passages between island groups.

The standard shorthand from brokers: the **Western Med** (France, Italy, Spain) is glamour and events; the **Eastern Med** (Greece, Croatia, Turkey) is island cruising and quieter water. Neither is better overall.

Mediterranean quotes are normally in euros — at current rates, budget roughly $1.08 to $1.10 per euro, but confirm the rate your broker uses.

## Taxes and APA by country

Tax is where the regions genuinely differ, and it is added on top of the base fee:

- **Italy:** 22% VAT on the charter fee (published BoatCrowd sample).
- **France:** 20% tax (published BoatCrowd sample).
- **Croatia:** 13% charter tax (published BoatCrowd sample) — the lowest of the three, which is part of Croatia's value case.
- **Greece:** no reliably sourced figure available to us; ask your broker for the current position in writing.

APA sits on top of tax: typically 25 to 40% of the base fee (YachtCharterFleet), with motor yachts nearer 35% and many crewed catamarans nearer 25% (LuxuryCharterGroup). The APA is a deposit against actual running costs, not a fee — unspent money comes back.

## Choosing by trip style

Forget "best region" rankings. Match the region to the trip:

- **Choose France** for glamour, events and short hops: marquee harbours, beach clubs, people-watching. Book early — dates and yachts go first here.
- **Choose Italy** for culture plus coastline: Amalfi's villages, Sardinia's anchorages, Sicily's history. The most varied single-country itinerary.
- **Choose Croatia** for relaxed island-hopping and value: a thousand islands, calm water, historic towns, and the lowest charter tax of the three priced regions.
- **Choose Greece** for island variety and space: distinct island groups, clear Aegean anchorages, a stronger sense of discovery — but be honest about pace, because passages between groups are longer.

Also consider winds, which shape routes more than brochures admit: the **Meltemi** blows in the Aegean in summer (strong but steady, good for sailing yachts, choppier for light motor yachts), and the **Mistral** can disrupt plans off the French and Italian coasts. Your captain plans around both; just avoid building a tight itinerary that assumes flat seas every day.

## Seasons

The Mediterranean season runs roughly **May to October**, with a clear structure (sources: Moran Yacht & Ship, Burgess, Ahoy Club, Ocean Breeze Charters):

- **May to June:** warm weather, fewer crowds, excellent availability, lower prices. Ideal for relaxed cruising and island-hopping at its most serene.
- **July to August:** peak season — hot (25–35°C), vibrant, busy marinas, world-famous events, highest prices. Book months ahead.
- **September:** warm seas, fewer crowds, relaxed pace. Many experienced charterers call it the sweet spot.
- **October:** the tail of the season; pleasant but less predictable weather, thinner availability.

Shoulder months (May, early June, September) consistently offer the best value: comfortable temperatures, berth availability and lower rates than high summer.

## A sample 7-day pace

Most Mediterranean charters last about a week — enough for one region without rushing. A realistic pace, whatever the region:

- **Day 1:** embark late afternoon, short hop to a nearby anchorage, dinner on board.
- **Days 2–5:** one or two stops per day — a morning swim anchorage, lunch ashore or on board, an afternoon harbour or town. This is the rhythm the whole week should keep.
- **Day 6:** position back toward the disembarkation port; final dinner.
- **Day 7:** disembark mid-morning after the APA accounting.

The classic first-timer mistake is four countries in seven days. The Med rewards slow: fewer stops, longer swims, unhurried lunches. Ten days or more opens up wider cruising areas or a genuinely slow week; anything under a week and you spend the whole trip in transit.

Tell your broker your preferred pace before they plan the itinerary — "two stops a day maximum" is a perfectly good brief, and it is much easier to set expectations now than to renegotiate mid-charter.
