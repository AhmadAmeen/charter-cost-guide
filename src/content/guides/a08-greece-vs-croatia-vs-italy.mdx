## Costs and taxes

This is where Croatia and Italy can be compared properly, because the published samples use the same yacht size band (80 to 100 ft) and the same provider.

- **Croatia:** base fee samples of $41,000 to $94,000 per week, APA about 35%, charter tax about 13%.
- **Italy:** base fee samples of $42,000 to $80,000 per week, APA about 35%, VAT about 22%.

Work the tax into your mental total early. On a $60,000 base fee, Croatia's 13% adds $7,800 while Italy's 22% adds $13,200 — a $5,400 swing on tax alone, before any difference in the base fee itself. Both totals then still need APA (about 35% in these samples) and a crew tip of around 10%.

For Greece, no comparable published sample was available to us. That does not mean Greece is more or less expensive — it means you should get written quotes for your specific dates and compare them against the Croatian and Italian samples above.

## When to go

Season affects both price and experience, but the details differ by country and even by coastline within a country. Rather than memorising month charts from a third party, ask your broker three questions:

1. Which weeks do you treat as peak season for this yacht and region?
2. How does the base fee change between peak and shoulder weeks?
3. What is the weather and sea-state trade-off in the shoulder weeks?

Peak weeks bring the warmest water and the busiest anchorages; shoulder weeks often bring lower rates and quieter ports. Neither is right for everyone — match the season to your group's tolerance for heat, crowds and the occasional unsettled day.

## Coastline and trip style

Numbers aside, the three destinations cruise differently:

- **Croatia** suits travellers who want many stops with short passages: the Dalmatian coast packs islands closely together, so you can swim, eat ashore and move on without long days at sea.
- **Italy** mixes culture and coast: historic ports, the Amalfi Coast, Sardinia and Sicily. Itineraries often blend sightseeing days with anchorage days.
- **Greece** offers the widest island variety of the three, spread across distinct island groups. The trade-off is distance: moving between groups means longer passages, so be honest with your broker about the pace you want.

None of these is objectively "better" — they suit different trips. A family with young children and a group of experienced sailors will read the same coastline very differently.

## Practicalities

A few practical points that apply whichever you choose:

- **Season length and peak pricing** differ by country; ask your broker which weeks they consider peak for your destination.
- **Marina infrastructure** is well developed along all three coasts, but remote Greek island anchorages can be simpler — part of the appeal for some, a drawback for others.
- **Embarkation ports** affect the price: starting outside a yacht's home port can add a delivery fee. Ask whether your preferred start port is the yacht's base.
- **One-way itineraries** (embark in one port, disembark in another) are possible in all three countries but may carry repositioning fees.

## Money practicalities across the three

A few financial details that are easy to overlook when comparing destinations:

- **Quote currency.** Mediterranean quotes are commonly issued in euros and Caribbean quotes in US dollars, but either can appear. When comparing a Croatia quote against an Italy quote, make sure you are comparing in one currency — and note the date of any conversion you do yourself.
- **Where the APA is held and in what currency.** Confirm this before you transfer funds; exchange-rate movement between booking and embarkation is your risk unless the agreement says otherwise.
- **What "plus expenses" includes.** The boundary between included costs and APA spending is set by the individual agreement, not by the country. Two Croatian quotes can differ on this — read both.
- **Tipping custom.** Around 10% of the base fee is the commonly cited Mediterranean guidance; on very large yachts the percentage often comes down. When in doubt, ask your broker what is customary for the yacht size you are considering.

None of these should be deal-breakers for a destination — they are simply lines to confirm in writing before you compare totals.

## Crewed or bareboat: does the destination matter?

Both formats exist in all three countries, but the practical questions differ:

- In **Croatia**, bareboat charter is well established and licence requirements are clearly documented — confirm yours matches the cruising area before booking.
- In **Italy**, longer distances between some cruising grounds make the crewed-versus-bareboat decision partly about how much daily passage-making you want to do yourself.
- In **Greece**, the longer passages between island groups mean bareboat crews should be honest about their experience level; a hired skipper (about 180 to 250 euros per day, single-source 2026 guide figure) is a middle path worth costing.

If you are torn between the two formats, the rule is simple: match the format to your licences, your experience and the pace you want — not to the destination's reputation.

## Who each destination suits

Use this as a starting point for the conversation with your broker, not a verdict:

- **Consider Croatia first** if you want the most predictable budgeting (clear published samples, moderate 13% tax), short passages and a high density of stops.
- **Consider Italy first** if your trip blends sailing with onshore culture — historic ports, celebrated coastlines and islands like Sardinia and Sicily — and you accept the higher 22% VAT line in the budget.
- **Consider Greece first** if island variety is the priority and your group is comfortable with the occasional longer passage between island groups. Get written quotes early, since published cost samples are thinner here.

All three reward the same preparation: identical briefs to two or three brokers, line-by-line comparison, and the tax line read before the total.

## How to choose

1. **Rank your priorities.** If budget predictability matters most, Croatia and Italy give you the firmest published numbers. If island variety matters most, Greece deserves the quotes.
2. **Fix the trip first.** Guest count, dates and flexibility, then destination — the same order as our [first-timer's guide](/yacht-charter/how-to-charter-a-yacht/) recommends.
3. **Get comparable quotes.** Send two or three brokers the identical brief for each destination you are considering, and compare them line by line with our [quote checklist](/checklist/).
4. **Read the tax line.** Confirm exactly how charter tax or VAT is calculated for your itinerary before you compare totals across countries.

Whichever coast you choose, the budgeting method is the same: base fee, plus APA, plus tax, plus tip. Our [cost guide](/yacht-charter/cost/) walks through the arithmetic with three worked examples.
