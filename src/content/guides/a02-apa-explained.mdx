APA is the part of a yacht charter quote that confuses first-timers most — and it is also the part that causes the most end-of-trip arguments. It stands for Advance Provisioning Allowance: a deposit you pay before the charter that covers the trip's running costs. This guide explains what it is, what it covers, the typical percentages, and how the money is accounted for.

If you have not read our [yacht charter cost guide](/yacht-charter/cost/) yet, start there for the full picture of base fee, APA, tax and tip. This page goes deep on the APA layer alone.

## What APA is

The APA is an upfront estimate of what you will spend during the charter, paid before embarkation — usually at the same time as the final charter balance. The captain uses it to pay for fuel, provisions, dockage and similar running costs as the trip unfolds.

Three facts matter most:

- **It is your money, not a fee.** The crew spends it on your trip's costs, at cost. It is not profit for the owner or the broker.
- **It is an estimate.** The percentage is a planning figure based on the yacht type and your itinerary. A busy itinerary with long daily passages can push spending above the estimate.
- **It is standard on plus-expenses charters.** Most crewed charters are quoted this way: a base fee for the yacht and crew, plus APA for everything the trip consumes.

Before the trip you also fill in a preference sheet — food preferences, dietary requirements, favourite drinks — so the crew can provision to your tastes. The more specific and premium your requests, the higher the APA estimate may run.

Before the trip you also fill in a preference sheet — food preferences, dietary requirements, favourite drinks and brands — so the crew can provision to your tastes. The more specific and premium your requests, the higher the APA estimate may run: vintage wines and specialty foods cost more than standard provisioning, and the system is designed to absorb exactly that kind of customisation.

## What it covers — and what it does not

APA covers the variable costs of operating the yacht on your itinerary:

- **Fuel** — for the yacht and the tender, including fuel for water toys.
- **Food and drink** — provisions bought to your preference sheet.
- **Dockage and marina fees** — nightly berthing, mooring balls, port fees.
- **Incidentals** — national park fees, customs charges and similar items depending on the cruising area.

It does not cover:

- **The base charter fee** — that is a separate payment.
- **Local taxes or VAT** — added on top, varying by country.
- **Crew gratuity** — commonly handled separately; confirm with your broker how the tip is paid on your charter.
- **Delivery fees** — if you want to start somewhere other than the yacht's home port, moving the yacht there is billed separately and is explicitly not part of APA.

<Callout type="note" label="At cost, no markup">
One point worth knowing: the extras paid from APA — food, fuel, drink — are charged to you at cost, without markup. The system exists so the crew can buy what your trip needs, not to create a profit line.
</Callout>

## Common APA misunderstandings

- **"APA is a fee."** No — it is your money, spent at cost on your trip's running costs. A fee would not come back; unspent APA does.
- **"Unspent APA is lost."** No — it is returned with receipts. What you should verify is the refund's timing and method, not whether it happens.
- **"APA covers everything extra."** No — base fee, taxes, gratuity and delivery fees sit outside it. Read the agreement's line items, not just the APA percentage.
- **"The percentage is fixed."** No — 25 to 40% is the published range, but your broker sets the figure for your yacht and itinerary. A quiet week at anchor and a 500-mile cruise should not carry the same estimate.

## Unspent and overspent APA

**Unspent APA is returned to you.** The captain keeps an account of APA spending during the charter, and at the end presents the balance with receipts; anything unspent comes back. Before you book, ask your broker two questions and get the answers in writing: who holds the APA funds, and exactly how is the unspent balance refunded (timing and method)?

**Overspent APA is settled by you.** If spending exceeds the allowance — unusual on a normal itinerary, but possible with heavy fuel use or premium requests — the difference is billed separately, usually settled on board or shortly after. The captain should warn you if spending is running hot during the trip, not surprise you at the end.

<Callout type="tip" label="Tip">
Ask at embarkation how APA spending will be reported to you during the trip. Many captains give running updates; if yours does not offer, ask. A mid-trip check avoids any end-of-trip dispute.
</Callout>

## All-inclusive vs plus-expenses charters

Crewed charters are quoted in one of two ways, and mixing them up makes quotes impossible to compare:

- **Plus expenses:** the base fee is quoted on its own; fuel, food, dockage and similar costs are paid from your APA during the trip. This is the model behind every example on this page, and the most common quoting style.
- **All-inclusive:** most costs are bundled into a single rate. It looks simpler, but read the terms carefully: premium beverages, special catering requests, dockage above a set allowance and crew gratuities are frequently still excluded.

Neither style is automatically cheaper. Convert both to an estimated all-in total — base fee, plus APA or its bundled equivalent, plus tax, plus tip — and compare those totals. Our [hidden costs checklist](/yacht-charter/hidden-costs-checklist/) walks through every line to check.

## APA vs the security deposit

APA is sometimes confused with the security deposit (also called a damage deposit). They are separate line items on your agreement. The APA pays your trip's running costs and is spent during the charter; the security deposit is refundable money held against damage to the yacht, returned if the yacht comes back undamaged. Some yachts offer a non-refundable damage waiver instead of a deposit. A quote that mentions neither deserves a question before you pay anything — see our [yacht charter cost guide](/yacht-charter/cost/) for how the deposit fits the full budget.

## Regional differences in APA

The 25 to 40% range is global; regions have their own norms, driven mostly by fuel costs on typical itineraries:

- **Greece:** commonly cited at 25 to 40% of the charter fee (2026 Greece charter cost guide).
- **Caribbean (BVI):** one BVI charter guide cites 20 to 40% of the base rate.
- **Grenadines vs elsewhere:** BoatCrowd's regional samples list APA at about 25% in the Grenadines versus about 35% in Italy, France, the Bahamas and Croatia — reflecting lower fuel burn on typical Grenadines itineraries.

The pattern is consistent: where itineraries burn less fuel, APA runs lower. Your broker should set the percentage for your route, not copy a global average.

## Questions to ask your broker

- What APA percentage are you setting for my itinerary, and why?
- Who holds the APA funds, and how is spending reported to me during the trip?
- How and when is unspent APA returned, and what receipts do I receive?
- What happens if spending exceeds the APA — how is the difference settled?
- Is the crew gratuity paid from APA or separately?
- Are there any delivery fees or repositioning charges outside the APA?

A broker who answers these clearly, in writing, before you pay the balance is a broker you can trust with the rest of the booking.
