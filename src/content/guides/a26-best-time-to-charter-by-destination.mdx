Timing is the single biggest lever on a charter holiday after the choice of yacht. The same boat in the same place can cost a third less — and feel twice as pleasant — if you pick the right month. The Mediterranean and the Caribbean run on opposite calendars, which is convenient: when one is at its best, the other is usually at its cheapest or its quietest.

New to chartering entirely? Read our [first-timer's guide to chartering a yacht](/yacht-charter/how-to-charter-a-yacht/) first, then come back and pick your month.

## How season affects price

Three things move together through the year: weather quality, demand, and price. Peak months combine the best conditions with the highest rates and the fiercest competition for the best yachts. Shoulder months trade a little weather certainty for meaningfully lower prices and quieter anchorages. Off-season means the lowest prices but real compromises — closed seasonal businesses in the Med, hurricane risk in the Caribbean.

Published sources agree on the shape of this curve even when they differ on exact month boundaries. In the Caribbean, shoulder-season weeks are cited at roughly 30 to 40% below peak rates, with off-season discounts of 30 to 50% (source: LuxuryAchtVacation, September 2026). In the Mediterranean, July and August command the premium while May, June, September and October price below peak (sources: YachtCharterFleet; Northrop & Johnson).

Demand also decides what is actually available to buy at any price. Peak weeks do not just cost more — the best yachts and crews go first, which is why every broker guide repeats the same booking windows: 6–12 months ahead for high season, 10–12 months for Caribbean holidays (sources: GetBoat; LuxuryAchtVacation). A shoulder-season booking is therefore doing double duty: lower rates and a wider choice of boat.

## Mediterranean season

The Mediterranean charter season runs roughly **May to October**. Within that, published guides describe it this way:

- **Peak: June to September.** YachtCharterFleet puts the peak at June through September, with July and August the busiest and most expensive weeks as European summer holidays fill every berth. September stays warm with fewer visitors.
- **Shoulder: May and October.** Quieter months suited to sightseeing and gastronomy; sea temperatures are cooler in May and October can bring variable weather, particularly in the northern Med (source: YachtCharterFleet).
- **Off-season: November to April.** Most of the fleet repositions or goes into winter refit; seasonal restaurants and beach clubs close. November to April is also when the lowest rates appear, for the few yachts that stay available (source: GetBoat charter seasons guide).

One broker guide aimed at catamaran charterers frames it slightly differently — peak July–August, shoulder May–June and September–October, early season April–May — which shows why the grid below cites per-row sources rather than pretending to a single authority (source: SongsInTheSails).

Regional winds matter too: the Meltemi blows in the Aegean (especially the Cyclades) through summer, the Mistral can hit southern France and the western Med, and the Bora affects the Adriatic (source: YachtCharterFleet). If you are sensitive to wind, ask your broker which cruising ground is calmest in your month.

East versus west is worth knowing as well. The season stretches longer in the eastern Med: YachtCharterFleet notes that warm temperatures extend well into October in destinations like Croatia, Greece and Turkey. If you want late-season swimming without the August crowds, the east gives you more calendar to work with than the French Riviera.

## Caribbean season and hurricane season

The Caribbean charter season runs roughly **mid-November through July**, and it is the mirror image of the Med (source: LuxuryAchtVacation):

- **Peak: December to April.** Steady northeast trade winds at 15–25 knots, rainfall of two to four inches a month, temperatures of 75–84°F. February and March are cited as the finest months of all — the driest weather and most dependable winds. Christmas and New Year weeks are the most sought-after of the year and often book 10 to 12 months ahead (source: LuxuryAchtVacation).
- **Shoulder: May, June and November.** Roughly 80–90% of peak conditions at significantly lower cost, with quieter anchorages. November is singled out as a standout month as hurricane season winds down; May and June bring the warmest water of the year (source: LuxuryAchtVacation).
- **Off-season: August to October.** The lowest prices of the year, but this is hurricane season and most operators reposition their fleets. Summer charters stay viable south of the main hurricane belt — the ABC islands, Trinidad and Tobago, Grenada, Barbados and St. Lucia (source: LuxuryAchtVacation).

There is a mechanical reason the Caribbean calendar looks the way it does: fleet positioning. Most crewed charter yachts spend winter in the Caribbean and summer in the Mediterranean, crossing the Atlantic in November and returning in May or June. That migration is why the widest selection of yachts and crews is available December through April — the boats are simply there (source: VitalCharters, September 2026). Book outside that window and you are choosing from whichever fleet stayed behind.

**Hurricane season** officially runs from **June 1 through November 30** each year (source: NOAA, May 2026 outlook), with climatological peak activity around September 10. Note that charter-season sources and storm-season sources overlap in June, July and November — those months are genuinely transitional, and the grid marks them as such.

## Shoulder-season trade-offs

Shoulder season is where experienced charterers get the most for their money, but it is a trade, not a free lunch:

- **Weather risk rises slightly.** May in the Med can still feel cool for swimming; October can turn unsettled, especially in the north. November in the Caribbean sits at the tail of hurricane season.
- **Some services are reduced.** Seasonal beach clubs and restaurants may not all be open in May or October in the Med.
- **Choice is better, not worse.** With fewer charterers competing, you get a wider pick of yachts and easier marina reservations — crews often call May–June and September–October the best time of year for exactly this reason (source: SongsInTheSails).
- **Events cluster in peak.** If your trip is built around an event — Antigua Sailing Week in April, the Monaco Grand Prix in May/June, Les Voiles de Saint-Tropez in September — the calendar chooses for you.

## Planning tips

- **Book peak weeks 6 to 12 months ahead.** Mediterranean July–August and Caribbean Christmas/New Year go first; one Med broker guide recommends 6–10 months for peak catamaran weeks, and Caribbean holiday weeks are often committed 10–12 months out (sources: GetBoat; BoatAndSailboat; LuxuryAchtVacation).
- **Be flexible on the exact week.** Shifting by one week either side of a peak boundary can move you from peak to shoulder pricing.
- **Plan around events if they matter to you.** The Monaco Grand Prix and Cannes Film Festival in May, the Les Voiles de Saint-Tropez regatta in September (all flagged on YachtCharterFleet's Med guide) and Antigua Sailing Week in April in the Caribbean (source: LuxuryAchtVacation) each fill their weeks and price at peak regardless of the surrounding month. If events do not matter to you, avoid those weeks.
- **Ask the broker, not the brochure.** "Peak" varies by cruising ground — the Balearics peak differently from the Ionian. Ask which weeks the operator prices as high season for your specific itinerary.
- **Insure the weather risk.** If you book hurricane-season months in the Caribbean, get trip-interruption cover and read the operator's storm policy before you pay.
- **Pair the calendars.** A Med charter in September followed by a Caribbean charter in February gives you two peak experiences in one year — this is exactly how much of the charter fleet itself moves.
